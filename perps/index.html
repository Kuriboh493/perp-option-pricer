What the model assumes

  1. Perp fair value. Two funding rules. Linear: funding accrues at κ(F − S) + ιS, so F = S(κ − ι)/(κ − (r − q)), the risk-neutral spot sampled at an exponential time of mean 1/κ (Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann). Clamped: each interval longs pay ι + soft(p − ι, γ) on the premium p = (F − S)/S, flat inside the clamp, so no arbitrage puts the basis at p = c ± γ, c being the carry per interval (He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter). The clamp is why a perp can sit a few basis points from spot for weeks.
  2. Perp drift. Because the perp tracks spot, its risk-neutral forward is F·e^((r−q)T). It is not a martingale like a dated future, so Black-76 with the mark as forward is slightly off.
  3. Jump diffusion (default). Merton (1976): spot is lognormal with constant diffusion volatility between jumps; jumps arrive as a Poisson process with lognormal sizes, and the drift is compensated so the forward is unchanged. The price is the Poisson-weighted sum of Black prices with variance σ²τ + nδ². The smile comes only from the jumps: mean jump sets the skew, jump size dispersion and frequency the curvature. Jump risk is priced as if diversifiable.
  4. Stochastic volatility (optional). Switching the model to Bates adds Heston variance dv = κv(θ − v)dt + ξ√v dW on top of the same jumps, priced from the closed-form characteristic function with one numerical integral (Lewis, 2001). In the Deribit backtest it fit smiles better in sample but forecast no better out of sample, so the simpler model is the default. Parameters are taken as already risk-neutral in both.
  5. Market hours. The variance process runs on a vol clock in which a closed hour counts as a fixed fraction of an open hour, scaled so a full week equals a calendar week. Jumps arrive on calendar time. Holidays are ignored.
  6. Everlasting options. A margined contract paying κ(mark − payoff) every period Δ. Its no-arbitrage price is Σ κ(1+κ)^−(n+1) E[payoff at nΔ], an undiscounted basket of European payoffs with geometric weights and mean horizon Δ/κ (Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann, Theorem 6). κ = 1 gives the original 2^−i weights.
  7. Settlement. Linear USD-margined perp. European options: premium paid up front in USD, exercise against the perp mark. No fees, no liquidation or oracle risk, flat rates.

References: Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann, Perpetual Futures Pricing, Mathematical Finance (2025). He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter, Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures (2025). Kim & Park, Designing funding rates for perpetual futures (2025). Bankman-Fried & White, Everlasting Options (2021).
